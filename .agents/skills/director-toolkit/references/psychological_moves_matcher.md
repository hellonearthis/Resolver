# Psychological Moves Matcher & Counter-Intuitive Directing Guide

A diagnostic engine connecting the internal psychological tension of a scene to the **Named Moves Bank** in `director-toolkit`. It enables creators and agents to move past cliché, on-the-nose cinematic staging by leveraging **Psychological Inversions**—directorial choices that feel counter-intuitive on the surface, but hit dramatically harder because of cognitive contrast.

---

## 🏛️ The Core Directorial Inversion Law

> **The Inversion Law:** *When the emotional stakes are violent, chaotic, or catastrophic, the camera, lighting, and blocking should become unnervingly controlled, quiet, or mundane. When the scene is low-stakes and trivial, the visual grammar can become absurdly escalated.*

On-the-nose direction matches sadness with blue rain and crying close-ups.  
Psychologically resonant direction matches sadness with an unmoving, brightly lit pastel room where someone sits in deadpan silence for eight seconds.

## 🎯 The Directorial "Grill-Me" Decision Sequence

When running a `/grill-me` session to match moves for a scene, follow this 3-question sequence on the Decision Frontier:

### Turn 1: Unearthing the Hidden Subtext (The Psychological Core)
*Never ask for camera specs first. Ask what is being suppressed beneath the spoken words:*
```text
### Frontier Decision: The Core Psychological Undercurrent
What is the true unsaid tension operating beneath the dialogue in this scene?

- (Recommended) Option A: Fragile Denial & Containment. They are acting as if everything is completely normal, but one slip will shatter the illusion.
- Option B: Asymmetrical Status Dominance. One character enters believing they hold total power, while the other is quietly waiting to spring a trap.
- Option C: Institutional Powerlessness. Characters are dwarfed by a system, machine, or corporate apparatus that does not care if they live or die.
- Option D: Intimate Exhaustion. Two people who love each other but are too emotionally depleted to maintain social masks.
```

### Turn 2: Conventional vs. Counter-Intuitive Staging (The Aesthetic Approach)
*Probe whether the user wants direct emotional alignment or an outside-the-box psychological inversion:*
```text
[Settled]: Psychological undercurrent is Fragile Denial & Containment.

### Frontier Decision: Directorial Posture & Inversion
How should the visual grammar treat this denial?

- (Recommended) Option A: The Counter-Intuitive Inversion (Wes Anderson / Pastel Armor). Use rigid dead-center symmetry and high-key pastel lighting. The hyper-manicured perfection makes their suppressed grief feel 10x more fragile.
- Option B: The Kitchen-Sink Pressure Vessel (80s Broadcast Restraint). Stationary camera, amber tungsten practicals, long unhurried pauses, and tight focus on mundane displacement (stirring tea, folding paper).
- Option C: The Claustrophobic Squeeze (Optical Creep). Start wide and comfortable, then slowly compress the focal length so the background walls physically close in on them.
```

### Turn 3: The Environmental Shift Catalyst (The Reactive Hook)
*Determine what physical event in the room breaks the tension:*
```text
[Settled]: Visual posture is Pastel Armor with rigid dead-center symmetry.

### Frontier Decision: The Environmental Catalyst
What physical disruption in the space shatters their fragile order?

- (Recommended) Option A: The Background Arrival. In the deep background through symmetrical glass double doors, a third figure silently appears, holding a Manila envelope. Foreground continues speaking without turning.
- Option B: The Dropped Score. A whimsical vintage European pop track plays continuously, then cuts out on a single frame mid-line, leaving dead silence and a buzzing refrigerator.
- Option C: The Physical Object Failure. A symmetrical ceramic teacup cracks cleanly in half in the character's hand, spilling tea across the pristine white tablecloth.
```

## 💡 Quick Rules for Outside-the-Box Directing
1. **Never match mood with obvious color:** Don't put depression in cold blue or happiness in sunny yellow. Put depression in over-saturated sterile pastels; put love in a dim, flickering industrial corridor.
2. **Never move the camera when you can move the lens:** A physical camera push feels like a director walking into the room. An optical zoom feels like a character's brain narrowing into obsession.
3. **Let the sound do the violent work:** Keep the visual shot deadpan and stationary while an aggressive acoustic sound (a heavy pneumatic door, an alarm, a sudden dropped score) shatters the scene.
