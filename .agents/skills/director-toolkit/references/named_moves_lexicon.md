# Named Moves Lexicon — Director Toolkit Reference

A modular bank of named cinematic techniques. Instead of imitating a specific film or director, reference these techniques directly by name in shot lists, screenplays, and AI video prompts.

---

### 1. The Held Wide
* **Camera:** Extreme wide or wide shot on a stationary tripod.
* **Technique:** The shot is held for 4 to 8 seconds *after* dialogue or physical action concludes. No cutting to a reaction close-up.
* **Psychological Effect:** Imposes dreadful inevitability, profound isolation, or deadpan comic absurdity. The audience is forced to sit inside the empty room.

### 2. The Symmetrical Dead-Center
* **Camera:** 35mm lens, camera precisely leveled on tripod, subject aligned dead-center on the central vertical axis.
* **Technique:** High-key lighting, one-point perspective with vanishing lines converging behind the subject.
* **Psychological Effect:** Evokes total institutional authority, brittle psychological denial, or surreal storybook order.

### 3. The Sodium Flicker
* **Lighting:** Unstable, buzzing low-pressure sodium vapor lamp (589nm deep amber-yellow) flickering erratically against cold cyan ambient darkness.
* **Technique:** Casts deep amber specular highlights across metal and wet concrete, intermittently dropping the frame into near-blackness.
* **Psychological Effect:** Industrial menace, dereliction, vulnerable human flesh exposed to failing, indifferent machinery.

### 4. The Bureaucratic Fluorescent
* **Lighting:** Flat, overhead 4300K fluorescent troffers with greenish cast, casting dark sockets beneath the eyes and washing out skin tones.
* **Technique:** Zero cosmetic fill light; accompanied by an audible 50Hz/60Hz ballast hum.
* **Psychological Effect:** De-individualization, sterile bureaucratic cruelty, institutional apathy.

### 5. The Optical Creep
* **Camera:** Stationary pedestal camera equipped with a motorized parfocal zoom lens.
* **Technique:** Slow, imperceptible zoom-in (e.g. 35mm to 95mm) across 6 to 10 seconds without physically moving the camera body. Background compresses and optical breathing occurs.
* **Psychological Effect:** Psychological claustrophobia; the exterior world is squeezed out of existence, trapping the character in internal realization.

### 6. The 14mm Intimate Grotesque
* **Camera:** 14mm to 18mm ultra-wide prime lens placed 12 to 18 inches from the actor's face.
* **Technique:** Low or canted camera angle exaggerating nasal bridge, forehead, and ear geometry while throwing the background into deep, distorted perspective.
* **Psychological Effect:** Manic paranoia, mental unraveling, comical or terrifying alienation from reality.

### 7. The Background Arrival
* **Blocking:** A tense, low-key conversation plays in medium two-shot in the foreground/midground.
* **Technique:** A third character, vehicle, or hazard enters in the deep background through an open door or fog. The foreground characters continue speaking, unaware of the intrusion.
* **Psychological Effect:** Sudden dramatic irony; shifts the audience from following dialogue to anticipating catastrophic confrontation.

### 8. The Dropped Score
* **Sound:** Rich musical score (orchestral or ambient synth) plays continuously throughout a sequence, and is abruptly cut to dead air on a single frame.
* **Technique:** Exposes raw, hollow domestic room tone (faint refrigerator hum, clock tick, solitary breath).
* **Psychological Effect:** Instant emotional rupture; signals that a lie has collapsed, a boundary has been crossed, or real danger has entered.

### 9. The Mundane Displacement Hold
* **Camera:** Camera slowly tilts or pans down from the speaking characters' faces to linger in macro close-up on their hands.
* **Technique:** An unhurried shot of fingers obsessively folding a paper receipt twice, stirring cold tea, or turning a wedding ring.
* **Psychological Effect:** Metaphoric displacement (the Objective Correlative); reveals immense internal pressure channeled into physical friction.

### 10. The Dutch Status Inversion
* **Camera:** Camera starts perfectly level during an argument where Character A dominates.
* **Technique:** At the exact moment Character B delivers an unexpected truth or power play, the camera subtly tilts 15° on the Z-axis.
* **Psychological Effect:** Visually announces the sudden collapse of institutional or interpersonal control.

### 11. The Anchor Replacement (The Stålenhag Pivot)
* **Mise-en-scène / Staging:** Staging a sequence across an open, undifferentiated landscape (winter field, barren highway, empty desert).
* **Technique:** Deliberately replace generic natural composition anchors (a bare tree, a boulder, a fence post) with monumental, world-building techno-relics (a rusted neuro-relay tower, an overturned drone fuselage, a severed industrial piston).
* **Psychological Effect:** Transforms empty geographical space into an unmistakable narrative anchor; gives the audience an immediate spatial compass while infusing mundane desolation with retro-futuristic weight.

### 12. The Persistent Background Micro-Narrative
* **Blocking & Directing:** During a chaotic foreground sequence (combat, panic, intense conversation), assign a distinct, tragicomic physical task to an identifiable background character or robot.
* **Technique:** Maintain this secondary micro-arc consistently across multiple cuts and camera setups (e.g. a damaged bot persistently trying to screw its head back on, or a security drone wandering through distant frames with a ski pole permanently stuck through its visor).
* **Psychological Effect:** Deepens world density and realism; rewards secondary visual inspection and prevents action backgrounds from dissolving into generic, ungrounded motion blur.

### 13. Zero-Cheating Battle Mapping
* **Layout & Previs:** Establishing a rigid top-down spatial map (grease-pencil dailies grid, digital layout pawns) prior to shooting coverage.
* **Technique:** Tracking character vectors, lines of sight, and spatial zones strictly across all angles and editorial recuts. If Character A moves east, they must physically occupy the background of Character B's reverse shot when looking east.
* **Psychological Effect:** Eliminates spatial disorientation and jarring continuity teleportation in complex multi-character clashes, preserving the tactile physical reality of the battlefield.

### 14. The Seesaw Handoff (Practical Grounding to Digi-Double)
* **Production Staging / VFX Integration:** When staging impossible physical interactions between human actors and massive mecha/creatures (e.g. an actor being lifted onto a giant robot's shoulder).
* **Technique:** Use a simple practical mechanical lever or seesaw rig on real sod/ground against container bluescreens to capture authentic human muscular weight, foot friction, and dynamic center of gravity before handing off to matchmove and a full digital double.
* **Psychological Effect:** Preserves the subtle micro-tremors and natural physics of human biomechanics, preventing the "weightless float" common in fully synthetic CGI action.

### 15. Improvised Mechanical Weaponization
* **Action Choreography:** In robot/mecha combat, characters repurpose severed mechanical components or enemy hardware dynamically rather than relying on standard firearms or punches.
* **Technique:** A combatant catches a flying drone out of mid-air and uses its spinning carbon rotor blades as a handheld rotary buzzsaw to cleave through an opposing bot.
* **Psychological Effect:** Generates tactile, kinetic surprise and mechanical grit, emphasizing industrial brutality and spontaneous physical problem-solving over choreographed martial arts polish.
