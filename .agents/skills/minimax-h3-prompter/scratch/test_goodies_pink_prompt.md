# MiniMax H3 Production Brief: The Goodies "Pink is a Colour" (20s Doco Scene)

## 1. Reference Job Assignments
- Image 1 (Optional Keyframe): 1970s BBC educational television studio, featuring a madcap British scientist with messy hair and thick black-rimmed glasses in a white lab coat, standing beside a wooden lab bench with retro oscilloscopes, a hand-drawn cardboard rainbow diagram, and glass beakers filled with vibrant neon pink dye.

## 2. Scene / Format / Mood
1970s British public television educational documentary parody in the visual and comedic style of 'The Goodies'. 16mm film stock with warm nostalgic color saturation, authentic vintage optical grain, slight gate weave, and soft edge halation. Setting is a cluttered, low-budget university laboratory studio with dark teak wood paneling, an overhead projector displaying a hand-drawn prism diagram, vintage analog cathode-ray monitors, and retro scientific apparatus. The mood is manic, eccentric, deadpan academic seriousness colliding with absurd slapstick comedy.

## 3. One Dominant Action
A quirky 1970s British scientist delivers an intense, fast-paced direct-to-camera lecture on why pink is a real color, gesturally pointing with a wooden pointer at a rainbow diagram before aggressively slamming an oversized red laboratory switch on his bench.

## 4. Camera Path & Framing
The shot opens in a steady medium close-up of the scientist behind the laboratory desk. As his delivery intensifies, the camera executes a continuous, slow optical push-in toward his face, tightening into a dramatic close-up. At the final punchline, the camera smoothly pans slightly right and snaps wider to capture the entire desk as he slams the lever, maintaining continuous 24fps motion blur and realistic depth of field without any jump cuts or synthetic wobbles.

## 5. Lighting & Palette
Authentic 1970s television studio 3-point tungsten lighting with warm key lights (3200K) casting soft defined shadows across the wood paneling. The laboratory backdrop features muted 1970s earth tones—mustard yellow, avocado green, and chocolate brown—which dramatically contrasts with the vivid, saturated hot-pink liquid glowing inside the glass flasks on the workbench.

## 6. Sound Clause
- [0s-4s]: Ambient 1970s studio room tone, low mains hum (50Hz), and rhythmic chalk tapping. The scientist speaks in a sharp, clipped British academic accent: "Pedantic boffins claim pink isn't a real colour because it has no wavelength on the light spectrum."
- [4s-14s]: Scientist's voice crescendos into manic conviction: "Absolute poppycock! Light has red at one end, violet at the other. When both smack your retina at once, your brain refuses to see a black void, panics, and violently invents pink out of pure neural spite!"
- [14s-17s]: Scientist points directly down the lens with wide eyes: "Colour is in the brain! Pink is real, it is scientific, and it is mandatory!"
- [17s-20s]: Heavy metallic clunk of a giant switch being thrown [17.5s], followed by a loud comedic high-pressure pressurized siphon hiss [18s] as bubbling neon pink foam explodes violently into the air, accompanied by a sudden short retro brass sting on trombones and kazoos [18.5s-20s].

## 7. Final Beat / Composition
The shot resolves on a wide static composition of the scientist covered in dripping hot-pink foam, staring expressionlessly into the camera lens while holding the wooden pointer upright amidst rising steam and bubbling pink beakers.

## 8. Negative Directions
No modern digital HD clarity, no CGI sheen, no digital anamorphic flares, no modern clothing or contemporary lab equipment, no bracketed timecode overlays, no floating text or subtitles, no extra fingers, no facial morphing during speech, no camera teleportation or sudden jump cuts.
