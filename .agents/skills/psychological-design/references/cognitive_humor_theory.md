# Cognitive Humor Theory — Psychological Design Reference

A technical reference mapping the psychological mechanisms that make humor work in the reader/audience's mind, rather than on the page. Designed for cross-referencing with `comedy-craft`, `comedy-tv-writer`, `mitch-hedberg-humor`, `steven-wright-humor`, and `inclusive-comedy-guardrails`.

---

## 1. Incongruity Resolution Theory (The Cognitive Engine of Jokes)

The dominant academic model of humor processing. A joke works in two cognitive steps:

1. **Frame Construction (Setup):** The reader builds a mental model — a "schema" — based on the most obvious reading of the setup. This happens automatically and unconsciously.
2. **Frame Violation + Resolution (Punchline):** The punchline violates the schema, but in a way that *retroactively makes sense* under a second, non-obvious reading. The surprise + coherence together produce the humor response.

### The Critical Distinction: Surprise Alone Is Not Funny
Random nonsense violates expectation but provides no coherent second frame to resolve into. The reader must be able to "get it" — to see, in retrospect, that the second reading was available all along. This is why the **Dual-Validity Rule** in the wordplay system works: both readings must be genuinely valid.

### Implications for Writing
* **Setup economy:** The setup's job is to prime Frame A as strongly and efficiently as possible. Every word should deepen the wrong assumption.
* **Pivot sharpness:** The switch from Frame A to Frame B should be instantaneous — ideally on the terminal word (the "kick word"). No run-up to the punchline.
* **Retroactive coherence:** After the punchline, the reader should be able to re-read the setup and see that Frame B was hiding in plain sight.

## 2. Benign Violation Theory (McGraw & Warren, 2010)

A complementary model: humor occurs when something is simultaneously perceived as:
1. **A violation** — something wrong, threatening, or norm-breaking.
2. **Benign** — something safe, playful, or non-threatening.

Both conditions must be active *at the same time*. If only violation is present, the response is offense, disgust, or fear. If only benign, there's nothing to laugh at.

### The Three Benign-Violation Routes
| Route | Mechanism | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Psychological distance** | The violation happened long ago, far away, or to a fictional character | "Comedy is tragedy plus time" |
| **Alternative interpretation** | A second reading makes the violation harmless (connects to incongruity resolution) | Puns, double entendres |
| **Weak commitment to the violated norm** | The audience doesn't deeply hold the norm being violated | Satirizing a rule everyone already thinks is silly |

### Implications for Writing
* **Calibrate the violation intensity:** Too mild = not funny (the "so what?" response). Too strong = offensive (the norm matters too much to the audience). The skill is finding the sweet spot.
* **Control benignity through character:** A character who is clearly the architect of their own suffering (self-sabotage, comic flaw) makes the violation benign because the audience isn't asked to feel cruel.
* **This is the theoretical basis for "punch up, not down":** Punching down fails the benign test because the violation targets someone vulnerable, and the audience's empathy makes it feel genuinely threatening rather than playful.

## 3. Status Play & Sudden Inversion

Comedy is often the art of status shifting suddenly. Keith Johnstone (*Impro*) identified that audiences are exquisitely sensitive to relative status between characters, and that humor lives in the *transition* — the moment status flips.

### The Four Status Comedies
| Pattern | Mechanism | Classic Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High → Low (Deflation)** | Someone with unearned authority or pomposity is punctured | Basil Fawlty, David Brent, Sir Humphrey |
| **Low → High (Elevation)** | The underdog accidentally wins through literalism, calm, or dumb luck | Baldrick's cunning plan actually working, Manuel's obliviousness saving the day |
| **Status Blindness** | A character who cannot perceive the status shift happening to them | Alan Partridge, Uncle Obvious explaining physics wrong |
| **Mutual Destruction** | Both characters' status collapses simultaneously | Steptoe and Son, Bottom |

### The Psychological Hook: Identification + Schadenfreude
The audience identifies with the low-status character (or the part of themselves that fears being exposed as incompetent) while simultaneously enjoying the high-status character's deflation. This dual identification is what makes the status flip *feel* like something rather than just being observed.

## 4. Pattern Violation & The Rule of Three

The cognitive basis: two repetitions establish a pattern (the brain's prediction engine locks on), and the third instance violates it. The humor lives in the *gap* between what the brain predicted and what arrived.

### Why Three (Not Two or Four)?
* **Two:** Not enough data for the brain to commit to a prediction.
* **Three:** Minimum viable pattern + violation. The third beat carries maximum surprise because the brain has just committed.
* **Four+:** Diminishing returns — each additional beat weakens the pattern's predictive grip (unless you're deliberately escalating absurdity, in which case the pattern *is* the escalation and the violation is stopping).

### Deflation vs. Escalation
These are opposite applications of the same cognitive lever:
* **Deflation (Understatement):** Setup inflates the stakes → punchline treats it as trivially small. *The comedy is in the mismatch between the character's affect and the stakes.*
* **Escalation (Absurdism):** Setup introduces a small thing → each beat makes it more catastrophically enormous. *The comedy is in the mismatch between the triviality of the cause and the enormity of the consequence.*

Both work because the audience's expectation-calibration system is being deliberately miscalibrated.

## 5. The Cuzzy Obvious Paradigm: Wilful Understatement as Psychological Design

A specific application of deflation + status blindness that maps directly to the Uncle Obvious / Cuzzy Obvious character territory:

> **The comedy isn't in the words — it's in the mismatch between the character's affect and the stakes.**

The character states the catastrophically obvious with total calm, deflating tension that everyone else treats as huge. Psychologically, this works because:
1. The audience is primed to expect urgency (high-stakes frame).
2. The character responds with absolute pragmatic calm (violates the frame).
3. The resolution is that the character's reading is *also valid* — the obvious thing really is obvious, it's just that social convention forbids saying it plainly.

This is **incongruity resolution** (Frame A: "this is a crisis" → Frame B: "this is common sense") combined with **benign violation** (the violation is social — saying the quiet part out loud — but benign because the character means no harm) combined with **status inversion** (the "expert" is shown to be overcomplicating what the "amateur" can see clearly).
